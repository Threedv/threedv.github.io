Not a collection of unrelated papers. A proposed research loop in which assets and recovered motion support data, data supports reconstruction, and learned structure supports generation.

The scientific bridge

Humans and animals share questions about articulation, motion, and contact, but not identical anatomy. The long-term goal is to reconstruct both and understand their relationships within a shared scene.

The evidence gate

Recovered motion is an estimate, not real-world ground truth. Validate asset–motion compatibility first, then test whether the resulting data improves generalization on independent real observations.

03 / Research directions

Each direction starts from a concrete limitation and asks what a better representation would let us do.

I / AnimalAgentONGOING / ANIMAL ASSETS

An animal asset is not an endpoint.
It is a reusable research building block.

AnimalAgent is the animal-asset direction in this program. The aim is to build animatable bodies that can be paired with recovered motion, supporting data creation and later reconstruction and generation research.

Questions to investigate

  • What makes an animal asset useful beyond its original reconstruction?
  • Can recovered motion transfer without breaking articulation, proportions, or contact?
  • Which asset variations provide useful supervision for unseen animals?

What would count as progress?

One asset and one recovered motion that combine reliably enough to generate useful training examples. Scaling the asset library comes after validating that connection.

II / Animal MotionPUBLISHED FOUNDATION + ONGOING

Not just where an animal is.
How it moves through the world.

WildAni4D connects monocular video to temporally coherent animal meshes, articulated motion, and world-space trajectories. The broader question is how such observations can support reusable motion representations and controllable generation.

Questions to investigate

  • How do we separate body motion from camera motion and ambiguous observations?
  • Which motion structure transfers across animals with different proportions?
  • How can language and visual priors guide motion without losing anatomy or temporal consistency?

What would count as progress?

Motion that stays consistent over time and supports a new camera, body, or condition. Reconstruction fidelity, physical plausibility, and downstream usefulness should be evaluated separately.

III / Human Motion UnderstandingRELATED WORK + ONGOING

Understand the movement.
Preserve the interaction.

Human motion is more than a sequence of poses. I connect controllable avatars, pose analysis, and ongoing multi-dancer generation research with a broader question: how can we recover and understand human movement in relation to other people, animals, and the scene?

Questions to investigate

  • How can body identity and motion remain consistent under occlusion?
  • What changes when an action is defined by a human–human or human–animal interaction?
  • Which human motion priors transfer to animals—and which assumptions break?

What would count as progress?

Temporally stable bodies and coherent interactions under difficult observations. Related avatar and try-on publications are foundations, not claims of a completed motion-understanding system.

Questions worth staying with

05 / Across the three directions

When does explicit 3D / 4D earn its complexity?

When a body can be reused for a new viewpoint, motion, edit, or interaction. A better rendered frame alone is not the only test.

What should transfer between human and animal motion?

Shared ideas about articulation, temporal structure, and contact are useful. Morphology, anatomy, and behavior make blind transfer unreliable.

Can generative priors fill in evidence without inventing the body?

Generation can propose missing views and motion. The research challenge is to preserve identity, anatomy, and consistency while representing uncertainty.

How can we reconstruct both sides of an interaction?

A human and an animal should not be reconstructed as unrelated subjects. Their relative motion, contact, occlusion, and responses define a shared event. Modeling that relationship is the longer-term goal.
